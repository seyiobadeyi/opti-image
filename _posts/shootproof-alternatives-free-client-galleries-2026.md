**ShootProof's free plan gives you 100 photos and 5GB of storage — numbers that sound reasonable until you deliver your first wedding gallery and blow past both before you've even finished the edit.** ShootProof is a solid paid product once you're on one of its photo-count tiers, but the free plan is really a trial wrapped in permanent-sounding language. If you're not ready to pay $10-30 a month yet, or you just don't want to, here are five tools that handle client delivery without that ceiling.

## Why ShootProof's Free Tier Doesn't Hold Up

ShootProof's paid structure scales by photo count: $10/month gets you 1,500 photos, $20/month gets you 5,000 and adds invoicing and contract signing, $30/month gets you 25,000 photos with more business tooling on top. The free plan sits underneath all of that at just 100 photos and 5GB — genuinely useful for testing the interface, not for delivering actual client work. A modest engagement shoot alone can run 150-300 final images. A wedding gallery routinely runs 500-1,000+. The free tier isn't built to survive contact with a real job.

## The 5 Best Free ShootProof Alternatives

### 1. Optimage — Best for No Photo Count Ceiling

[Optimage](https://optimage.dreamintrepid.com) is a free, browser-based tool that includes [PIN-protected client galleries](/galleries) alongside a full image workflow — [compress](/compress), [resize](/resize), [convert](/convert), [crop](/crop), [watermark](/watermark), and [strip metadata](/metadata) — all in the same place you're already preparing the images. There's no per-photo cap tied to a paid tier and no account required to start. Clients get a PIN-protected link with full-resolution download access, which matters for anyone whose contract includes print-ready file delivery.

Best for: photographers who want gallery delivery and image prep in one free tool, with no photo-count wall waiting to force an upgrade.

### 2. Pixieset — Best for Built-In Print Sales

Pixieset's free plan includes 3GB of storage and unlimited galleries, plus a built-in storefront if you want to sell prints directly through the gallery. The catch is a 15% commission on any print sales made through that storefront on the free tier — reasonable if you're not selling prints yet, worth budgeting for if you are.

Best for: photographers who want an integrated print-sales storefront and don't mind a commission cut until they're ready to upgrade.

### 3. CloudSpot — Best Free Storage Allowance

CloudSpot's free plan gives you 10GB of storage — more than double Pixieset's — spread across up to 3 active galleries. The restriction is on the client side: free-plan galleries only allow web-size downloads, not full-resolution originals. Fine for social-sharing-focused clients, a dealbreaker if your contract promises print-ready files.

Best for: photographers who need generous storage and whose clients only need web-quality downloads.

### 4. Pic-Time — Best for High-Volume Studios Willing to Pay Early

Pic-Time doesn't offer a meaningful permanent free tier the way the others on this list do, but it's worth knowing about because its paid plans are built specifically around high-volume studios — AI-assisted culling, automated proofing workflows, and album design tools baked in. If you're past the free-tier stage entirely and shopping for a paid platform, it's a legitimate ShootProof alternative to compare against.

Best for: studios already paying for gallery software who want more automation than ShootProof offers at a similar price point.

### 5. SmugMug — Best for Photographers Who Also Want a Public Portfolio Site

SmugMug doesn't run a permanent free plan either, but it bundles client galleries with a full portfolio website and print fulfillment in a single subscription, which some photographers find cheaper than running separate tools for galleries and a public site. Worth a look if a standalone portfolio is on your to-do list anyway.

Best for: photographers who want their client galleries and public-facing portfolio site on one platform.

## Which One Should You Actually Use?

If you're delivering full-resolution files and don't want a photo-count ceiling forcing an upgrade mid-season, Optimage's free tier is the most direct replacement for what ShootProof promises but doesn't deliver at the free level. If print sales through the gallery itself matter more to your business than an unlimited photo count, Pixieset's commission-based free model is worth the trade. If your clients only need web-size downloads and you want the most free storage on the list, CloudSpot's 10GB wins on paper — just confirm that download restriction won't cause a problem with a client expecting prints.

The honest answer for most working photographers: start with a free tool that doesn't cap you on photo count, because that's the limit you'll hit first and fastest. Upgrade to a paid platform only once a specific feature — automated proofing, built-in invoicing, a public portfolio — is worth paying for on its own merits, not because a free plan forced your hand.
