## How the Match Actually Unfolded

Spain took the lead in the 23rd minute through Lamine Yamal, continuing the tournament-long form that turned him into the story of the semifinal against France. Messi answered in the 38th, pulling Argentina level before halftime. Spain regained the lead through Alvaro Morata in the 61st, and for the next eighteen minutes it looked like the storybook ending wasn't going to happen — until Messi scored again in the 79th, forcing extra time at 2-2. In the 106th minute, with the match delicately balanced and the clock working against both sides, Messi scored his third of the night to put Argentina ahead for the first time all match. Julian Alvarez added a fourth in the 118th minute, closing it out and sending Argentina into full celebration before the final whistle even sounded.

## Why This Match Breaks the "One Great Photo" Approach Completely

A match with two comebacks, a trailing team, and a hat-trick spread across regulation and extra time doesn't reduce down to a single defining image the way a comfortable 2-0 win might. There are at minimum five moments tonight that each deserve their own space in a recap: Yamal's opener, Messi's first equalizer, Morata's response, Messi's second equalizer forcing extra time, and Messi's extra-time goal that actually won it — before you even get to Alvarez's clincher or the trophy presentation itself. Treating any single frame from tonight as "the photo" of the match undersells how much the story actually swung.

1. **Group your photos chronologically by goal, not by which shot looks best in isolation.** The emotional arc — Spain ahead, level, Spain ahead again, level again, Argentina ahead — is the actual narrative, and it only reads correctly in sequence.
2. **Keep the tense, quiet moments alongside the celebrations.** The 18 minutes Argentina spent behind after Morata's goal are part of what makes Messi's second equalizer land as hard as it does. Don't cut straight from goal to goal and lose the tension in between.
3. **Don't discard your extra-time shots as an afterthought.** Messi's decisive goal came in the 106th minute, well past where a lot of casual photographers stop paying close attention, assuming the match is winding down.
4. **Separate your "history" shots from your "match" shots.** Messi's hat-trick closing out his final World Cup appearance is a career-spanning story, not just a match recap — caption and organize it that way if you're building anything more permanent than a quick post.

Mbappé's brace in last night's third-place match had put him two goals clear of Messi entering today, meaning Messi needed a big night just to catch up, let alone win outright. A hat-trick did both in one match — first drawing level at 10, then pulling ahead to 11 with the extra-time goal, closing the individual race in the same 120 minutes as the trophy itself.
