**The tournament is over, and if you followed it across the US, Mexico, and Canada, your photos are almost certainly split across a mess of different apps, local SIM cards, and cloud accounts that were active at whatever stage of the trip you were on.** This is a genuinely different organizational problem than a single-city event creates, and it's worth solving properly now, while you still remember which photo goes with which match.

## Why a Three-Country Tournament Creates a Worse Mess Than Usual

Switching networks and, for many fans, local SIM cards between host countries means your phone's default backup and sharing behavior wasn't consistent throughout the trip. Photos shared over a local messaging app in one country might have been compressed differently than ones backed up to cloud storage in another, simply because of which apps were active, which network was available, and what settings were default in each place. The result, weeks later, is a collection where photo quality and organization vary depending on which leg of the trip a given image came from — not because of anything you did wrong, just because of how differently each stage of a three-country tournament actually worked on the ground.

1. **Gather everything into one place first, before editing anything.** Pull photos and videos off every device, messaging app, and cloud account that was active at any point during the trip. This step alone usually surfaces photos you'd forgotten existed.
2. **Normalize quality across the whole set.** A photo saved through a compressed messaging app thread from the group stage and a full-resolution shot from the final look jarring side by side without running the whole set through one consistent compression pass.
3. **Label while the details are still fresh.** Match, opponent, stage, city — this is the step everyone puts off and regrets within a month, once the specifics of which photo came from which game start blurring together.
4. **Build a shareable gallery**, not just a folder. If you traveled with anyone or want to share the collection with people who followed along remotely, a proper gallery beats a zip file or a scattered group chat thread.

## Do This Before It Becomes Next Tournament's Problem Too

Every World Cup this happens again for anyone who follows more than one — the pile of unsorted photos from this year joins whatever's still unsorted from the last one, and the job only gets bigger. Doing it now, while the tournament and its outcome are still fresh, is meaningfully easier than doing it during the buildup to whatever comes next.
