**Move-in weekend is happening right now at campuses across the country, and the single most skipped step in the entire process is also the cheapest one: photographing the room completely, before a single box goes in.** It sounds like overkill for a room you're about to fill with your own stuff anyway. It isn't. Every year, a predictable number of students get hit with a deposit deduction for damage that was already there when they arrived — a scuff on the wall, a chip in the desk, a stain on the carpet — because nobody photographed the room's actual starting condition and the housing office's own move-in inspection notes are, generously, inconsistent.

## Why This Specific Documentation Gap Costs Real Money

Most colleges do some version of a move-in condition report, filled out by an RA or housing staffer walking through with a checklist. In practice, that inspection is often rushed — one staffer covering dozens of rooms in a single afternoon during the chaos of move-in weekend, checking boxes on a form rather than genuinely documenting condition. When checkout rolls around at the end of the year and a damage charge shows up, the burden of proof effectively falls on the student to show the damage predates their occupancy. A vague memory of "I think that mark was already there" loses that argument every time. A photo with a visible timestamp, taken the day you moved in, wins it immediately.

This isn't a hypothetical worst case — it's routine enough that housing offices themselves sometimes recommend it, buried in move-in paperwork most students don't read closely during the chaos of the actual day.

## The Ten-Minute Checklist

1. **Photograph the room fully empty, before a single box or bag comes in.** Every wall, the floor, the closet interior, the desk and drawers — a general walkthrough set, not just one shot from the doorway.
2. **Get close-ups on anything that's already imperfect.** A scuff, a nail hole, a worn spot on the carpet, a chip on the desk edge — these are exactly the items that show up on a checkout damage list, and a clear close-up dated to move-in day is the only evidence that actually holds up.
3. **Photograph shared spaces separately from your own side of the room**, if you have a roommate — bathroom condition, shared closet space, common areas. Splitting a damage charge with a roommate over something neither of you can prove predates your arrival is a genuinely common and avoidable conflict.
4. **Check that your phone's date and location settings are accurate before you start.** A photo's embedded timestamp is part of what makes it credible evidence later — if your phone's clock is wrong, fix that first.
5. **Get everything off your phone and into a backed-up location the same day.** A photo that only exists on a phone you might lose, break, or upgrade before checkout in nine months isn't documentation you can actually count on.

## Why This Matters More for Roommates Than People Expect

The financial deposit angle gets most of the attention, but the roommate-dispute angle is just as common and often more stressful in the moment. Two students moving into a shared room, each with their own sense of what the space looked like on day one, is a setup for exactly the kind of low-stakes-but-genuinely-annoying disagreement that photo documentation ends instantly. "Was that stain on the carpet before either of us moved in, or did one of us cause it" is an unanswerable question nine months later without a photo, and a completely avoidable argument with one.

## Get It Backed Up Properly, Not Just Taken

Taking the photos is half the job — having them somewhere retrievable when you actually need them nine months later, at checkout, is the other half. A folder buried in a camera roll that's since filled up with a full year of unrelated photos is nearly as bad as not having taken them.

1. **Create one dedicated folder or album for move-in documentation**, separate from your general phone photos, so it's still findable at the end of the year.
2. **Compress the batch for easy backup and sharing** — full-resolution originals aren't necessary for documentation purposes, and [a compressed set](/compress) uploads faster and takes up less storage without losing the detail that actually matters for proving condition.
3. **Share a copy with a parent or send it to yourself via email or a gallery link**, so it survives even if your phone doesn't make it to May.

## Do It Before You Unpack a Single Bag

If move-in is happening this weekend, take the photos first — before the boxes, before the decorations, before the excitement of settling in takes over. [Batch-compress and back up your move-in photo set](/compress) the same day, and you'll have real proof of condition sitting ready if you ever need it at checkout.
