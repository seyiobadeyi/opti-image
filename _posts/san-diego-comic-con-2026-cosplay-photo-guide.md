Overhead fluorescents, warm booth spotlighting, and colored LED accents from nearby displays are all fighting for the same frame, and most cameras' automatic white balance simply averages them into a muddy compromise. Add a moving crowd and costumes that range from matte fabric to reflective armor, and you've got one of the most technically demanding casual photography environments most people encounter all year — worse than a wedding, worse than most sports.

## Shooting: What Actually Helps in the Hall

1. **Set white balance manually for whichever light source dominates that section of the hall**, rather than trusting auto — a booth with strong spotlighting needs a different setting than open floor under fluorescents.
2. **Shoot in RAW if your camera or phone supports it** — it gives you real recovery room for the inevitable exposure mistakes mixed lighting causes, which a compressed JPEG straight out of the camera doesn't.
3. **Get close enough that costume detail actually registers** — armor seams, face paint blending, fabric texture are the entire point of a cosplay photo, and they disappear first at a distance in a crowded hall.
4. **Shoot more frames than feels necessary and cull hard afterward** — with people moving through frame constantly, your best shot of a costume is rarely your first attempt.

Most cosplay photos don't lose detail in the camera — they lose it the moment they get sent through a messaging app or uploaded straight to social media without a deliberate compression pass first. Platform and app default compression is tuned for average photos, not for images dense with fine fabric weave, painted texture, and small prop detail, which is exactly the kind of content most compression algorithms flatten hardest. A manual compression pass, where you control the quality target instead of accepting whatever the app decides, preserves noticeably more of that detail at a comparable file size.

1. **Resize to the platform's actual display size before compressing** — a smaller starting resolution compressed carefully looks better than a full-size photo left to an app's aggressive auto-compression.
2. **Use a quality setting in the 80-85% range for detailed costume shots** rather than accepting a platform's default, which is usually tuned lower for average phone photos.
3. **Batch-process the whole day's shoot at once** using [Optimage's bulk compressor](/compress) instead of exporting photo-by-photo between panels.

## What to Take From This

Comic-Con's exhibit floor produces some of the most detailed, effort-filled photo subjects you'll encounter anywhere, and the lighting and crowd conditions make it genuinely hard to shoot well. Get white balance right for the moment, get close enough for detail to register, and compress deliberately rather than letting an app decide — that's the entire difference between a cosplay photo that does the costume justice and one that turns hours of craftsmanship into a muddy, blurry post.
