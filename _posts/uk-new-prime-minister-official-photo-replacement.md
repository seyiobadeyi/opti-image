**Andy Burnham became the UK's seventh prime minister in just over a decade this week after Keir Starmer resigned, and while the political story dominates coverage, there's a quieter, genuinely large task now underway across the UK government's entire digital footprint: replacing an outgoing leader's photo everywhere it appears.** It's not a story anyone covers, but it's a real operational problem, and it happens every single time this kind of transition occurs, at every level of government worldwide.

An outgoing prime minister's official photo doesn't live in one place. It's on the Number 10 homepage, on every department's "our leadership" page, on embassy and high commission websites in dozens of countries, in printed and PDF briefing documents, in press kits, and often in the header or footer template of pages that have nothing directly to do with the PM's office but reference the sitting leader by convention. Seven changes in a decade means this exact scramble has now happened seven times, and there's no evidence any single shared system handles it cleanly across every department and mission at once — each one typically manages its own site, its own image assets, and its own update schedule.

## Why This Is Harder Than "Swap the Photo"

Replacing one image file is trivial. Replacing the same photo consistently across an ecosystem of pages maintained by different teams, in different formats, at different resolutions, updated on different schedules, is not. A department that hasn't updated its leadership page in two years might not even have a clear internal owner for the task now that it needs doing urgently. An embassy site running on older infrastructure might have the outgoing leader's photo hardcoded into a template rather than pulled from a central asset, meaning a "quick fix" actually requires a code change, not just a file swap. Multiply that across every department and mission that references a head of government, and a one-photo change becomes a genuinely large, decentralized project with a real risk of stragglers — a stale photo lingering on some minor archived page for months after everyone assumes the transition is complete.

## The General Lesson for Any Organization

This isn't just a government-scale problem. Any organization that changes leadership — a company replacing a CEO's headshot across its site, an association updating a board photo across chapter pages — runs into the same version of this task at a smaller scale: an official photo that exists in more places than any one person remembers, in inconsistent formats and resolutions, updated by whoever happens to maintain each individual page.

The fix that actually scales is a single source image, exported once at every resolution and format different pages need, distributed from one place rather than re-shot or re-cropped locally by each team maintaining a different page.

## What to Take From This

Political transitions get covered as political stories, which they are. Behind every one of them is also a quieter, genuinely large digital-asset problem that nobody writes about until a stale photo somewhere becomes an awkward, minor story of its own. Whether you're managing a government website or a company's leadership page, the actual fix is the same: one clean source image, distributed consistently, the moment a change happens rather than weeks after.
