**TikTok Shop just gave UK sellers a genuinely more useful analytics module, and for the first time it separates how a product performs by content format — livestream, video, or plain search discovery.** That last category is the one worth paying attention to, because it's the one where video and livestream can't compensate for a weak static photo. If a listing's search-discovery numbers are quietly dragging down an otherwise strong product, the fix almost never has anything to do with content strategy. It has to do with the main image.

## What Actually Updated

The Product Traffic Analysis module now shows product rankings by content format on the Product List page, plus a detailed breakdown of exactly how a product surfaces — through search, through the discovery feed, through a livestream, or through an affiliate link. Alongside that, TikTok Shop overhauled its Account Health Rating system this month: Negative Review Rate and Seller-Fault Return Rate are now scored against peers in the same product category instead of a flat universal threshold, and a new After-Sales Handling Time metric tracks how fast a seller resolves a return or complaint once it's opened.

## Why the Format Breakdown Actually Matters

Before this update, a seller could see total traffic and total sales for a product, but not which channel drove them. That flattened a real problem: a product might get most of its sales through a viral livestream clip while its static photo — the one that shows up in a plain search result with no video attached — quietly underperforms every time someone browses without watching a livestream. That gap was invisible in aggregate numbers. Now it isn't. A product with strong livestream numbers and weak search-discovery numbers has a specific, fixable problem: the main photo isn't doing its job when there's no motion or narration to carry the sale.

## What a Weak Search-Discovery Photo Usually Looks Like

1. **Shot at an angle that made sense on camera but reads flat as a static thumbnail** — livestream framing and product-photo framing aren't the same job.
2. **Compressed badly or oversized**, which either looks soft on a small feed thumbnail or loads slowly enough that a scrolling browser never waits for it.
3. **Missing the one visual detail that actually sells the product** — texture, scale, a size reference — because that detail was explained verbally during the livestream instead of shown in the photo.
4. **Inconsistent lighting or background from one listing to the next**, which reads as amateur next to competitors with a uniform, professional-looking catalog.

## Fixing It Without a Reshoot

Not every underperforming search photo needs a new photoshoot. Cropping tighter on the product, correcting exposure, and re-exporting at a size that loads fast on mobile fixes a surprising share of these cases without touching a camera again. ## What to Actually Do With This Data

Pull up the new traffic breakdown for your five best-selling products this week. Any listing where livestream and video traffic look healthy but search-discovery numbers lag is telling you something specific and fixable: the static photo needs work before the next promo push, not the video strategy.
