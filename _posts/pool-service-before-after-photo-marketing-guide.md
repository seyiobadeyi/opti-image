**Pool service companies are having their busiest stretch of the year right now — repeated heat waves have pushed even homeowners who'd been putting off cleaning or repairs to finally book, and every company in a given metro area is competing for the same booked-out demand through referrals and social proof.** In that environment, a before/after photo pair isn't just marketing decoration — it's frequently the deciding factor between a homeowner picking up the phone or scrolling past to a competitor's post. And a surprising number of otherwise solid pool companies are still shooting and posting those photos in a way that quietly undercuts them.

When demand outstrips availability, homeowners aren't researching carefully — they're comparing whichever two or three companies have availability and picking based on whatever convinces them fastest. That's almost always a Google Business Profile photo or an Instagram post, viewed on a phone, in the fifteen or so seconds before they move to the next option. A before/after pair that loads slowly, looks muddy, or doesn't line up cleanly between the two shots loses that comparison before the homeowner even reads a review.

## Shooting Pool Photos That Actually Hold the Comparison

Water is a genuinely harder subject than most trade photography. It reflects sky, nearby structures, and direct sun unpredictably, which throws off both exposure and color balance more than a dry surface like a driveway or a roof ever does.

1. **Shoot from a consistent angle and distance for both the before and after shot** — even a dramatic cleaning result looks unconvincing if the two photos don't visually align enough for a quick side-by-side comparison.
2. **Avoid direct midday sun glare on the water surface** — shoot at a slight downward angle, ideally earlier or later in the day, to reduce blown-out reflections that hide the actual water clarity you're trying to show.
3. **Keep the surrounding deck or landscaping visible in frame** — it gives a homeowner viewing the photo a fixed reference point to judge the change against, rather than just a patch of blue.

A phone camera's default photo is often 4-8MB, and a Google Business Profile or Instagram post loading two of those stacked side by side on a slow connection is exactly the kind of friction that loses an impatient homeowner mid-scroll. Compressing both photos down to a genuinely small file size, without visible quality loss at normal viewing size, fixes that without needing better source photos.

1. **Crop before/after pairs to matching dimensions and framing** so the comparison reads instantly.
2. **Compress both images together** using [Optimage's bulk compressor](/compress) so file size and quality stay consistent across the pair.
3. **Reuse the same compressed pair everywhere** — Business Profile, Instagram, a printed door hanger — instead of re-exporting inconsistently sized versions for each platform.

## What to Take From This

Booked-out demand doesn't mean marketing stops mattering — it means the comparison a homeowner makes between you and the next available company happens faster and more superficially than ever, usually on a phone screen in a few seconds. Shoot the before and after from the same angle, compress the pair deliberately, and that fifteen-second first impression works in your favor instead of quietly costing you the job.
