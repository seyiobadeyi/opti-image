**Emiliano Martínez won the Golden Glove again in 2026, and that makes him the first goalkeeper to win it in back-to-back tournaments — the same feat Kylian Mbappé pulled off for the Golden Boot in the same World Cup.** Two repeat individual awards in the same tournament, one for the best scorer and one for the best keeper, is genuinely unusual, and it's worth actually lining Martínez's run up against the keepers who've won this award before him.

Winning it once, in 2022, put him in the same company as three of the sport's most decorated keepers. Winning it again in 2026, after Argentina went all the way to a 4-2 extra-time final win over Spain, put him somewhere none of them had been.

## Why This Pairs With Mbappé's Golden Boot Run

It's worth noticing how unusual it is for both major individual awards to repeat with the same players in the same tournament. Mbappé's 10 goals matched Gerd Müller's 1970 tally for the Golden Boot, and he became the first back-to-back Golden Boot winner. Martínez did the same on the other end of the pitch. Two attacking and defensive individual records both getting broken by repeat winners, in the same World Cup, is the kind of statistical coincidence that doesn't happen often — the last time either award saw a repeat winner across the sport's full history was decades apart from each other, let alone in the same tournament.

## Shooting a Keeper's Best Moment

Goalkeeper action photography has a narrower timing window than almost anything else in the sport. A striker's celebration gives you a couple of seconds to react. A save gives you a fraction of one:

1. **The peak-extension frame is everything.** A dive photographed a half-second early shows a keeper still in motion, not yet at full stretch; a half-second late shows them already collapsing back to the ground. The shot that actually reads as a "great save" photo exists in a window of maybe two or three frames at typical burst rates.
2. **Keep the ball in frame.** A diving save photo without the ball visible near the keeper's hands loses the entire context — it just looks like an athlete falling down.
3. **Shoot from an angle that shows the full extension**, not straight-on. Side-on or a slight angle toward the goal line shows the actual distance covered, which is what makes a save photo look as difficult as it was.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who won the Golden Glove at the 2026 World Cup?

Emiliano Martínez won the Golden Glove at the 2026 World Cup, matching his 2022 award and becoming the first goalkeeper to win it in consecutive tournaments since the award adopted its modern format.

### Who has won the World Cup Golden Glove since 2010?

Iker Casillas won it in 2010 with Spain, Manuel Neuer in 2014 with Germany, Thibaut Courtois in 2018 with Belgium, and Emiliano Martínez in both 2022 and 2026 with Argentina — making him the only repeat winner in the award's modern history.

### What makes a goalkeeper save photo good instead of just lucky?

The best goalkeeper photos catch the dive at full extension, right at the peak of the save, with the ball still visibly in the frame near the keeper's hands. A half-second early or late and the shot either misses the athleticism entirely or just shows a keeper already back on their feet — it's a narrower timing window than most other action shots in the sport.
