**Boston's trade for Adley Rutschman was a real baseball move first — the Red Sox, winners of 23 of their last 26 games, needed an offensive upgrade behind the plate after Carlos Narvaez and Connor Wong combined for a .615 OPS, and a three-time All-Star switch-hitting catcher fixes that outright.** The return Baltimore got back was substantial too: pitching prospects Anthony Eyanson and Kyson Witherspoon, outfield prospect Enddy Azocar, catcher Narvaez himself, and a player to be named later. What doesn't show up in any of the trade-grade columns is the smaller, faster problem this created for Boston's media operation the moment the deal was announced: every photo, graphic, and roster reference showing Rutschman in an Orioles uniform was instantly wrong, and it needed to not be wrong within the same news cycle that broke the trade.

## Why a Catcher Trade Is a Harder Image Problem Than It Looks

A position player swap is already a photo-heavy event — new headshot, new jersey number, new roster-page thumbnail. A catcher trade specifically compounds it, because catchers appear in more distinct photo contexts than almost any other position: behind-the-plate action shots, mound-visit conference photos, pitch-framing highlight stills, and the kind of gear-heavy equipment photography that doesn't apply to an outfielder or a starting pitcher. Every one of those photo types that existed for Rutschman in an Orioles uniform is now a mismatch the moment he takes a Red Sox at-bat, and a fanbase that's been waiting on exactly this kind of upgrade is going to be searching for his new team photos within minutes of the trade going final — not next week.

That urgency is the actual constraint. A team's media department doesn't get a normal production timeline for a deadline-day trade; they get whatever window exists between the trade becoming official and the next game where Rutschman actually appears in the new uniform, which at trade deadline can be as little as a day or two.

## What Actually Has to Change, and Fast

A trade this size touches more of a team's digital footprint than most fans think about:

- **The active roster page and app** need Rutschman's headshot, bio, and stats swapped in immediately, with Narvaez's now needing to move to Baltimore's equivalent pages.
- **Depth chart graphics** — anything showing the catching rotation — need updating before the next broadcast, since a stale depth chart graphic airing on national TV during a big-name trade is exactly the kind of visible mistake that gets clipped and mocked.
- **Social media templates** built around the current roster, including any automated "starting lineup" graphic generator, need the new photo in the right uniform before the next game, not after.
- **Ticketing and promotional pages** that feature player photos for upcoming series need the swap too, particularly if Rutschman's arrival is being used to sell tickets to his first home series in the new uniform.

None of that is unusual work for a team's media department — it's the same category of task [covered for NFL roster cutdown day](/blog/nfl-roster-cuts-2026-headshot-photo-workflow-guide) and [NBA media day photo turnarounds](/blog/nba-blockbuster-trades-2026-media-day-photo-workflow). What's different about a trade-deadline swap specifically is the total lack of lead time — cutdown day and media day are scheduled events teams can prep for in advance; a deadline-day trade for a player this significant can land with essentially zero warning.

## The Practical Fix Is the Same Regardless of Sport

1. **Have a standard crop and compression setting ready before you need it**, not improvised under deadline pressure — a consistent output spec applied to a rushed photo shoot still looks professional; an improvised one usually doesn't.
2. **Batch-process every graphic that needs updating at once**, rather than fixing them one at a time as someone notices each is wrong — [resize and compress a full folder of new jersey photos](/resize) in one pass instead of exporting each individually.
3. **Get the new photo out fast even if it's not the final polished version.** A quickly-produced, correctly-uniformed photo beats an outdated one every time, and it can be swapped for a more polished shoot once there's actual time for one.

## Why This Particular Trade Will Get Referenced All Season

Rutschman is under contract through 2027, which means this isn't a rental — Boston's media team isn't producing a temporary graphic set for two months, they're building the visual identity around their new starting catcher for at least the next two seasons. Getting that first wave of photos right matters more than it would for a two-month deadline rental, because these are the images that'll anchor his Red Sox era from day one.
