**Most Amazon sellers don't lose sales because their photography is bad — they lose sales because their photo files are wrong for what Amazon's system actually does with them.** The photography can be genuinely excellent and still underperform if the file itself violates a spec buyers never see directly but Amazon's ranking and zoom features absolutely notice.

Here are the seven mistakes that show up constantly across Amazon seller accounts, in order of how much they actually cost you.

## 1. Uploading Camera-Original Files Instead of Optimized Ones

A modern mirrorless camera or even a flagship phone produces JPEGs in the 12-25MB range straight out of the shoot. Sellers listing 200 SKUs upload all of them at full camera resolution, thinking bigger means sharper. It doesn't. Amazon re-compresses everything on ingest and generates its own thumbnail and zoom tiers from your source file — a 20MB JPEG and a 2MB JPEG of equivalent visual quality produce an identical result on the buyer's screen. The only thing the 20MB file costs you is your own upload time, which matters a lot when you're updating 40 listings in one sitting through Seller Central's bulk image tool, which times out on oversized batches more than sellers realize.

## 2. Missing the 1000px Zoom Threshold

Amazon's product zoom feature — the one that lets a buyer click and inspect stitching, texture, or fine print before buying — requires the main image to be at least 1000x1000px, and Amazon's own documentation recommends 1600px+ on the shortest side for the sharpest zoom result. Sellers who shoot at exactly 800x800 to save file size are quietly disabling one of the few interactive features Amazon gives buyers to build purchase confidence. That's a real conversion cost, not a cosmetic one.

## 3. Off-White Main Image Backgrounds

Amazon requires the main image background to be pure white — RGB 255,255,255, not "close enough" white, not a light grey studio backdrop, not a slightly warm-toned white balance from indoor lighting. Listings with an off-white main image are technically in violation of Amazon's image guidelines and can get suppressed during a compliance sweep, which sellers often discover only when their listing quietly stops appearing in search. Check your main image background with a color picker tool before uploading, not just by eye.

## 4. Treating Slots 2-7 Like Duplicate Main Images

Sellers who nail the white-background main image often waste the remaining six slots repeating the same white-background shot from slightly different angles. Buyers who've already scrolled past the main image are looking for something the main image didn't answer: what does it look like in use, how big is it relative to a familiar object, what's in the box. Lifestyle and in-context images in slots 2 through 7 consistently outperform additional white-background repeats for both time-on-listing and conversion, because they answer the questions a buyer actually has left.

## 5. Wrong Aspect Ratio Cropping

Amazon's image container is effectively square, and non-square images get letterboxed or awkwardly cropped inside the listing thumbnail grid. A beautifully composed 16:9 lifestyle shot loses its framing entirely once Amazon fits it into a square container. Shoot or crop to 1:1 for anything going into the standard image slots, and save wider compositions for A+ Content modules, which support different aspect ratios than the core gallery.

## 6. Skipping Compression on A+ Content Modules

A+ Content (the enhanced brand content module) has a more generous file size ceiling than the standard listing gallery, and sellers take that as permission to upload full-resolution design exports without a second thought. That's backwards — A+ Content pages load as part of the listing page itself, and a bloated A+ module slows the whole listing down on mobile data, which is where a large share of Amazon browsing actually happens. Run every A+ image through compression the same as your main gallery images before uploading.

## 7. Not Re-Optimizing After a Reshoot

Sellers who reshoot a product for a refresh often export from Lightroom or Photoshop at a "safe" high-quality setting and upload directly, skipping the compression step they used to remember to do manually the first time around. Every reshoot deserves the same optimization pass as the original shoot — it's not a one-time setup task, it's a step in the workflow every time new images go up.

## The Fix Is a Workflow, Not a Reshoot

None of these seven mistakes require better photography. They require a consistent export step between "photo taken" and "photo uploaded to Seller Central." Shoot at 1:1, keep the main image on pure white, export at 1600px+ on the shortest side, and run the whole batch through [Optimage's bulk compressor](/compress) before touching the Amazon uploader — JPEG output at quality 85 keeps zoom sharp while cutting file size enough that a 200-SKU catalog update stops choking Seller Central's bulk tool.

## The Practical Takeaway

Good Amazon photography and good Amazon photo files are two different problems, and sellers who fix the second one usually see the improvement faster than they expect, because it's not competing with anyone's aesthetic taste — it's just removing friction between your product and Amazon's own zoom, search, and page-load systems.
