**Tropical Storm Dolly formed and dissipated over the Atlantic this week, but forecasters are already watching a new, well-organized tropical wave off West Africa with a high chance of development — right as hurricane season enters its historically busiest weeks.** For contractors and homeowners in storm-prone regions, this lull between storms is exactly the window to do the photo documentation that actually protects a claim, and it's a different job than the after-the-fact photos everyone remembers to take.

## Why This Particular Week Matters

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season started unusually slow — the slowest in nearly four decades by overall tropical activity, largely attributed to conditions tied to a strengthening El Niño pattern in the Pacific. That slow start doesn't mean a quiet season overall; it means the season's activity is compressed into a shorter window, and that window is opening now. Dolly's brief appearance and dissipation is a preview, not the main event — the tropical wave currently crossing the Atlantic from West Africa carries real development risk during exactly the stretch of late August into September when Atlantic hurricane activity climatologically peaks.

For anyone in a coastal or storm-exposed region, this is the calm-before-the-storm window in the most literal sense: enough warning that a real threat is developing, not so close that there's no time left to prepare documentation properly.

## Pre-Storm Documentation Solves a Different Problem Than Post-Storm Claims

Most homeowners think about roof photos only after a storm has already caused visible damage — cracked shingles, a torn gutter section, water stains spreading across a ceiling. That's a real and necessary step, but it answers only half the question an insurer actually needs answered: what's broken, and was it broken before the storm.

That second question is where a huge share of contested claims stall out. Without dated before-photos, an adjuster has no independent way to confirm that pre-existing wear, an old repair patch, or age-related deterioration wasn't already present before the named storm made landfall. Insurers are aware that claim timing creates an incentive to attribute pre-existing damage to a recent storm, and adjusters are trained to look for exactly that ambiguity. A homeowner or contractor with a dated, systematic before-set removes the ambiguity entirely, and claims move faster and with fewer disputes as a direct result.

## What a Proper Pre-Storm Documentation Set Looks Like

1. **Wide shots of every roof plane**, taken from ground level with a zoom lens or, ideally, drone footage for a full overhead view. Every slope, not just the ones visibly worn.
2. **Close-ups of flashing, vents, ridges, and valleys** — the specific points where leaks and wind damage most commonly originate, and where insurers focus scrutiny during a claim review.
3. **Gutter and downspout condition**, including any existing sagging, rust, or detachment from the fascia, since gutter damage is one of the most commonly disputed claim categories.
4. **Any visible prior repairs or existing wear**, photographed honestly rather than avoided. A documented pre-existing issue is far less of a liability than an undocumented one that surfaces during a claim dispute.
5. **A timestamp and location tag on every photo**, either through the camera's built-in metadata or a dedicated documentation app, so the dating of the entire set is independently verifiable rather than resting on the homeowner's word alone.

## Storing It Somewhere That Survives the Storm Itself

A documentation set that only lives on a phone's camera roll defeats its own purpose if that phone is lost, damaged, or simply inaccessible during an actual evacuation. Back the full set up to cloud storage or email it to a second account immediately after taking it — the point of pre-storm documentation is that it exists independently of whatever happens to the property or the devices in it during the actual event.

Compress the full photo and any drone footage set before backing it up. A complete roof documentation pass, especially with drone footage included, easily runs into hundreds of megabytes at full resolution — compressed appropriately, the same coverage backs up in a fraction of the time and space, which matters when preparation time before a real storm threat is often measured in days, not weeks.

## For Contractors: This Is a Service Worth Offering Proactively

Roofing, gutter, and exterior contractors in storm-prone markets have a natural opportunity here: offering a pre-season documentation service, not just post-storm repair and claim support. A contractor who already has dated, professional photos of a client's roof condition from before a storm hit is positioned to move directly into repair work with a documented baseline already established, rather than starting the claim process from scratch after damage occurs.
