**Argentina play Spain in today's World Cup final at MetLife Stadium, kickoff 3 p.m. ET, and it's Messi's last World Cup match no matter what happens on the pitch.** That fact changes what's actually worth photographing today. Most people treat kickoff as the starting line for their camera roll. The better plan starts hours earlier, because the moments most likely to matter in five years aren't all going to happen between the whistle and the final substitution.

## Why Today Isn't Just Another Match Day

Earlier in this tournament, a comeback win against Egypt got captioned as a possible farewell before the result made that framing wrong — Argentina won, and Messi kept playing. That correction doesn't apply today. Whatever happens against Spain, this is genuinely the last World Cup match of his career, which means the emotional stakes of every angle — walking out of the tunnel, standing for the anthem, whatever expression crosses his face in the final minute — carry weight a regular knockout match doesn't. It also means Mbappé's brace in last night's third-place match, which put him two goals clear in the Golden Boot race, adds a second storyline running underneath the main one: Messi needs goals today for more than one reason.

1. **Arrival (roughly 1 p.m.–2:30 p.m. ET).** Team buses, fan gatherings outside the stadium, jersey-clad crowds streaming toward the gates — this is where the "this is a big day" context lives, and it's the footage that makes a recap feel like it was actually there rather than pulled off a broadcast feed.
2. **Anthem and tunnel walk-out (roughly 2:45 p.m.–3 p.m. ET).** The tunnel walk is the single most reliable moment for capturing an expression before the adrenaline of the match takes over. If you have any angle on the players' faces before kickoff, this is it.
3. **The match itself (3 p.m. ET onward).** Standard advice applies, amplified: shoot every shot on goal in burst mode, because you don't know yet whether the goal that matters most is a Messi strike, an Argentina winner, or a Spain answer to it.
4. **The walk-off, whatever the result.** A losing walk-off carries as much weight as a winning one on a day like this — arguably more, because there's no second chance to correct the mood in post the way you can crop a celebration photo into something cleaner.

## Don't Let the Golden Boot Subplot Eat the Whole Story

It's tempting to treat every Messi touch today as Golden Boot content given how tight the race is. Resist collapsing the whole match into stat-tracking — the actual trophy is the bigger story, and a photo set that's all numbers and no atmosphere reads thin later. Keep both threads going: shoot the match for the match, and separately note anything that changes the goal count, but don't let the arithmetic take over your shot list.
