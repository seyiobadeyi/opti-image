**Instagram's Your Algo feature is rolling out globally, and it flips a basic assumption creators have relied on for years: instead of an opaque algorithm deciding who sees your content, users are now hand-picking the topics that shape their own Reels feed.** That sounds like it should make quality matter less — if someone chose the topic, surely they're a captive audience. The opposite is closer to true, and it's worth understanding why before you keep exporting the same way you always have.

## What Your Algo Actually Changes

Users can now add or remove specific interest topics from what shapes their Reels recommendations, moving some of the ranking control away from pure behavioral signal (what you watched, for how long, whether you engaged) toward explicit user choice. It's part of a broader pattern this year of platforms giving users more visible control — Instagram is also testing paid perks tied to follower visibility and story-viewing privacy, part of the same push toward users feeling like they have a hand on the dial rather than being purely algorithmically managed.

## Why Manual Curation Raises the Bar Instead of Lowering It

The instinct is to assume a self-selected audience is a locked-in audience — they picked the topic, so they'll watch regardless of production quality. That's backwards. A viewer who actively opted into a topic is paying closer attention to whether content in that topic is actually good, because they're comparing every video against their own explicit interest rather than getting served content passively between things they didn't ask for. A soft thumbnail, a video that visibly recompressed into mush after three reposts, or a slow-loading cover image reads as a worse signal to someone who deliberately chose to see more of this kind of content than to someone scrolling on autopilot.

## What Creators Should Actually Do Differently

1. **Re-check your cover image quality, specifically.** A thumbnail is the first thing a topic-curated viewer judges against their expectation for that topic — a low-res or oddly cropped cover reads worse now than it did under a purely passive feed.
2. **Export video at the highest quality your account tier allows**, since repeated compression from re-uploads or screen recording degrades fastest exactly where a self-selected audience is looking closest — motion and fine detail.
3. **Don't assume niche content gets a pass on production value.** If anything, a smaller, more intentional audience for a specific topic is comparing you against the other creators in that same topic more directly than a general feed ever would.
4. **Keep a consistent visual style across posts in the same topic**, since users curating a specific interest are more likely to follow multiple accounts in it and notice inconsistency across them.

## The Practical Takeaway

Your Algo doesn't reward a "post more, worry less" strategy — it rewards creators who treat every post in a topic a user explicitly chose as competing directly against the best other content in that same topic. That means the export settings that were fine for a passive algorithmic feed deserve a second look now.
