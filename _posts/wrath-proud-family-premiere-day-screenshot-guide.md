**Netflix's new drama Wrath and the final season of The Proud Family: Louder and Prouder both drop today, and if you're planning to screenshot either one to share your reaction, you're dealing with two almost opposite compression problems.** Wrath is shaping up as a dark, moody drama — the kind of show that lives in shadow and low-key lighting. The Proud Family is flat, bright, heavily saturated animation. Both compress badly if you treat them the same way, just for completely different reasons.

## Wrath: The Shadow Detail Problem

Dark, moody dramas are consistently the hardest footage to screenshot cleanly. Standard image and video compression allocates fewer bits to shadow detail than to midtones and highlights, on the reasonable assumption that human vision is less sensitive to fine differences in near-black areas. That assumption holds up fine for typical footage. It falls apart for a show that spends most of its runtime in low light, because the compression is throwing away detail from the majority of the frame, not just the parts you'd expect to lose first. A screenshot from a scene like that, run through a platform's default compression, often turns fine shadow gradation into flat, blocky black well before anything in the midtones shows visible damage.

## The Proud Family: The Flat-Color Banding Problem

Animation with flat, heavily saturated color fields — exactly The Proud Family's house style — compresses more efficiently than live-action in general, since there's less fine texture detail to preserve. But it introduces its own failure mode: push the compression too hard and large flat color areas start showing visible banding, faint stepped lines where a smooth gradient should be. It's a subtler problem than Wrath's shadow crush, but just as noticeable once you know to look for it, especially in gradient backgrounds behind character close-ups.

## Cropping Out a Spoiler Doesn't Remove It From the File

Whichever show you're screenshotting, cropping a frame to hide a spoiler in the visible image doesn't touch what's stored around it. A screenshot still carries a capture timestamp in its metadata, and if you're pulling frames from a downloaded episode, the filename or folder structure often preserves the exact scene or timestamp context even after you've cropped the visible spoiler out. If you're sharing final-season Proud Family reactions or early Wrath reaction shots before everyone's caught up, [stripping the metadata](/metadata) before posting is the step cropping alone doesn't cover.

## Getting Screenshots From Either Show Right

1. **For Wrath and anything similarly dark**, compress conservatively — a higher quality setting than you'd use for a bright scene, since there's less headroom before shadow detail collapses.
2. **For The Proud Family and other flat-color animation**, watch for banding in backgrounds specifically, and back off the compression level if you see stepped gradients appear.
3. **Strip metadata on both** before sharing anything that could reveal plot details ahead of when your audience has actually watched it.

### Why do dark TV show screenshots lose detail faster than bright ones under compression?

Standard compression allocates fewer bits to very dark tonal ranges because the human eye is less sensitive to detail in shadow than in midtones and highlights. That's a reasonable tradeoff for most footage, but it means moody, low-light shows lose shadow detail into flat, blocky black well before a brighter scene from the same episode shows any visible compression damage.
