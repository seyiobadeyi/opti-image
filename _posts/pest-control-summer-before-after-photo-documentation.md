**Summer is peak pest season, which means pest control technicians are shooting more before/after documentation per week right now than at any other point in the year — and most of those photos are still going out as unedited, oversized phone shots that take forever to open in a customer's inbox.** The treatment itself is rarely the reason a customer disputes an invoice or skips the referral. The photo proof is.

## Why Photo Proof Carries So Much Weight in This Industry

Unlike a repair job where the customer can see the physical result — a fixed pipe, a patched wall — pest treatment's actual effectiveness is mostly invisible to the customer. They didn't watch the technician work, and they can't verify a termite treatment or a rodent exclusion job just by looking at the property afterward. The before/after photo pair is doing almost all of the evidentiary work that a visible repair would otherwise do on its own.

That makes the photo the actual deliverable in the customer's mind, even though the treatment is the billed service. A dark, blurry, or poorly framed "after" photo undercuts confidence in work that may have been done perfectly, simply because there's no other way for the customer to judge it.

Two things go wrong most often. First, the before and after aren't shot from the same angle or distance, which makes the comparison read as unconvincing even when the treatment worked well — a customer can't tell if the improvement is real or just a different camera position. Second, the photos are emailed or texted at full phone resolution, which for a modern smartphone can mean 4-8MB per image. That's slow to attach, slow to send over a spotty job-site connection, and slow to open on the customer's end — exactly the moment they're deciding whether to trust the invoice.

## The Fix Is a Five-Minute Habit, Not New Equipment

1. **Mark the shooting position with something consistent** — a piece of tape, a fixed reference point in frame — so the before and after line up without needing to remember the exact spot later.
2. **Shoot both photos in the same lighting conditions** where possible. A before shot in harsh midday sun next to an after shot at dusk makes the comparison harder to trust, regardless of the actual result.
3. **Compress the pair before sending**, not after a customer complains about a slow-loading attachment. A same-day batch compression pass takes seconds per job and keeps every photo pair email-ready without manual resizing.
4. **Send the before/after as a clear side-by-side**, not two separate attachments the customer has to mentally compare — a combined image or a simple gallery link does more convincing work than two loose files.

## Why This Matters More During Peak Season Specifically

When job volume is high, the temptation is to skip the extra minute of photo handling to get to the next call faster. That's exactly backwards — peak season is when referral and repeat business compounds fastest, and a technician sending clean, fast-loading, well-framed before/after photos on every job builds a portfolio of proof that pays off for the rest of the year.
