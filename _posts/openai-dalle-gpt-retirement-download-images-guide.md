**OpenAI is retiring the official DALL-E GPT inside ChatGPT on August 30, 2026 — five days from today — and the company's own guidance is blunt: download anything you want to keep before then, because what happens to it afterward isn't confirmed.** This isn't the end of AI image generation in ChatGPT; ChatGPT Images continues and custom GPTs with image generation stay unaffected. But if you've been using the specific, original DALL-E GPT to make images over the past couple of years, that particular archive is on a five-day clock.

## Why This Deadline Is Easy to Miss

The DALL-E GPT has been sitting quietly in ChatGPT's GPT store for a long time, which is exactly why this is worth flagging now rather than assuming everyone already knows. It's not the interface most people use for image generation today — ChatGPT Images inside the main chat is — so a lot of the images actually at risk here were made a year or more ago, by someone who hasn't opened that specific GPT since. Old marketing mockups, a logo concept from a side project, birthday card designs made for a family group chat — the kind of thing you generated once, used once, and never went back to retrieve at full quality. Those are the files sitting in the DALL-E GPT's history right now, and "the company hasn't said what happens to them" is not a risk worth taking for anything you'd actually miss.

## What to Actually Do This Week

1. **Go through your DALL-E GPT conversation history first, not last.** It's easy to assume you'll get to this before the deadline and then not — treat it like any other five-day countdown and do the export early rather than on August 29.
2. **Download at full resolution, not a compressed preview.** DALL-E generations downloaded through a chat interface sometimes save at a lower resolution than the original render — check the file dimensions after downloading before you assume you have the best copy available.
3. **Batch-convert the whole set to one consistent format once you have them.** AI image generators often export as PNG by default, which is larger than it needs to be for anything you're not doing further editing on — [converting the batch to WebP or optimized JPEG](/convert) cuts storage and load time without losing visible quality for finished images.
4. **Compress before you re-upload anywhere else.** If you're moving these into a new tool, a website, or a shared folder, compress them as part of the migration rather than carrying over whatever file size ChatGPT originally generated — [Optimage's bulk compressor](/compress) handles a folder of AI-generated images the same as any other batch.
5. **Rename while you're at it.** AI generation tools hand back filenames like the platform's generic export string, not anything descriptive — this is the moment to fix that before the files scatter into a general downloads folder and lose all context.

## The Broader Pattern Worth Noticing

This is the second time in recent memory a major AI platform has sunset a specific image-generation surface while keeping the underlying capability alive elsewhere — the interface changes, the files you made with it don't automatically follow you. That's worth remembering any time you're using a specific AI tool's built-in storage as your only copy of something you'd actually miss: platform features get deprecated on the company's schedule, not yours, and "download it before you need it" is cheaper than finding out too late that a five-day notice period has already closed.

## Don't Let This Be the Last Time You Get Caught Out

If you're pulling images out of the DALL-E GPT this week, it's a good moment to also set up a real home for AI-generated work going forward instead of leaving it wherever it was made. [Compress and convert your downloaded set](/convert) into one organized batch, and if you're sharing any of it with a team or client, a [PIN-protected gallery](/galleries) beats emailing a folder of PNGs around one more time.
