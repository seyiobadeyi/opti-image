**Big Brother 28 premieres Thursday, July 9 on CBS, and the live feeds that make the franchise worth following for superfans start the very next night, Friday at 9 PM ET on Paramount+.** It's a milestone season — the 1,000th original episode in the show's history, with the "Time Trip" theme built around it — and Julie Chen Moonves is back to host. For anyone who's followed a Big Brother season before, there's one predictable side effect nobody warns you about: your phone's storage is going to take a real hit.

## Why Live Feeds Create a Screenshot Problem Nobody Plans For

Big Brother's entire appeal for its most dedicated fans lives in the 24/7 feeds, not the edited CBS broadcast. Feed watchers screenshot constantly — a strategy conversation in the have-not room, a blowup fight at 2 AM, a facial expression during a key vote — because feed moments disappear the second they happen and the only record is whatever you personally captured. Multiply that by a house full of feed-worthy personalities and a season that runs for months, and superfans routinely accumulate hundreds of screenshots a week during an active stretch. That's not an exaggeration; it's just what documenting a live, unscripted, 24-hour feed actually requires if you want receipts for later arguments about who said what.

## The One Thing Working in Your Favor: Screenshots Compress Extremely Well

Here's the good news feed superfans rarely think about: a screenshot of a live feed is mostly flat color, text overlays, and relatively simple visual information compared to, say, a photograph. That means screenshots are exactly the kind of image that compresses hardest with the least visible loss — you can shrink a folder of feed screenshots by 70% or more and still read every word of a house conversation clearly. Almost nobody does this, because saving a screenshot feels instant and "dealing with it later" feels optional, right up until "later" is a full phone during the week of a big blowup you desperately need to screenshot.

## Five Ways to Handle the Screenshot Flood This Season

1. **Set up one folder per week, not one giant camera roll dump.** "BB28 Week 1," "BB28 Week 2," and so on. When you inevitably need to find "the screenshot where Person A said the thing about the vote," a week-scoped folder search is minutes instead of scrolling through a season's worth of images.
2. **Batch-compress at the end of each week, not screenshot by screenshot.** Trying to manage file size in the moment defeats the point of a live feed — you screenshot fast because the moment is fast. Instead, once a week, drag that week's folder into [Optimage's bulk compressor](/compress) and let it shrink the whole batch in one pass.
3. **Convert your keepers to WebP if you're archiving long-term.** Screenshots you're keeping purely for personal reference — not re-sharing to Twitter or Reddit — do fine at a more aggressive compression setting than photos you want to look polished when reposted.
4. **Strip anything you're posting publicly through a quick pass first.** Feed screenshots occasionally catch identifying details in a background chat window or account notification most fans don't intend to share; a quick crop or blur pass before reposting avoids that.
5. **Don't wait until your phone warns you it's full.** That warning shows up mid-feed, mid-vote-week, at the worst possible moment to stop and clean up storage instead of watching what's happening in the house.

## Why This Season Specifically Will Test Everyone's Storage

A milestone 1,000th-episode season with a new "Time Trip" theme and a full slate of live feeds is exactly the setup that historically produces the most screenshot-worthy content — house twists, format changes, and a cast playing for a season the franchise is treating as a genuine landmark. Feed communities tend to document milestone seasons more obsessively than average ones, which means this year's screenshot volume is likely to run higher than a typical summer, not lower.

## The Takeaway

None of this requires watching the feeds any differently. It requires one small habit change: stop treating screenshots as a permanent dump into an ever-growing camera roll, and start treating them as a weekly batch you compress and archive on a schedule. By the time the season's first major blindside happens, you'll have room on your phone to screenshot it properly instead of deleting old photos in a panic mid-vote.
