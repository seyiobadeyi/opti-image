**Spain beat France 2-0 in the semifinal on July 14 — Mikel Oyarzabal converted a 22nd-minute penalty, Pedro Porro doubled the lead in the 58th — but the number that actually explains the match happened before kickoff: Lamine Yamal entered with 6 goal contributions this World Cup to Kylian Mbappé's 0.** Spain goalkeeper Unai Simón also set a World Cup record for clean sheets in the tournament, and France's overall attacking output was described afterward as the worst for a French side in six decades. This wasn't just a semifinal result. It was a stat comparison sharp enough that the photo worth keeping isn't a single action shot — it's a deliberate side-by-side.

## The Stat That Framed the Whole Match

Before a ball was kicked, the storyline around this semifinal wasn't really "Spain vs. France" — it was Yamal against Mbappé, one of the tournament's breakout attacking talents against its most established superstar. A 6-0 gap in goal contributions heading into a semifinal is the kind of number that turns a normal team-vs-team preview into a generational-torch-passing narrative, and the match result did nothing to undercut it. Add Unai Simón's record-setting run of clean sheets and a genuinely rough attacking night for France, and the numbers around this game tell a more complete story than the 2-0 scoreline alone.

## Building a Photo That Actually Tells This Story

A single celebration shot of Porro's goal doesn't communicate a generational stat gap — it just shows a goal. If you're building something worth keeping from a match framed this heavily around a comparison, treat it like one deliberately, the same way a comparison table works better than a paragraph for the same information.

1. **Shoot both players separately, at comparable moments — a warmup touch, a free-kick stance, a reaction to a chance.** Two photos of Yamal and Mbappé in visually similar poses side by side communicate the storyline faster than any action sequence.
2. **Capture the goalkeeper's body language after a save, not just the save itself.** A record-setting clean-sheet run is a composure story as much as a shot-stopping one.
3. **Get a wide crowd or scoreboard shot that includes the actual number** — final score, time remaining, anything that anchors the photo to the specific record-setting context rather than looking like any other match photo.
4. **Crop and arrange as an actual side-by-side afterward**, rather than relying on captions to do the comparison work a well-composed image pair can do visually.
