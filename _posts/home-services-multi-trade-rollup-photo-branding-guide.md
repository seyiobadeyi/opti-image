**Home services consolidation is moving fast this year — Blackstone's acquisition of Champions Group, Apollo's roughly $2 billion investment in Apex Service Partners, and this month's launch of JML Trades bundling plumbing, gas, air conditioning, and roofing under one brand are all part of the same trend.** Private equity sees a fragmented, aging-out industry ripe for consolidation, and multi-trade brands are the result. What almost nobody plans for in these deals is what happens to years of before/after marketing photos once five or six previously separate companies' websites get merged into one.

## The Photo Library Problem Nobody Budgets For

A typical roll-up combines companies that operated independently for years, sometimes decades, each with its own approach to documenting completed work. One legacy company might have used a professional photographer for its best jobs and a technician's phone camera for routine ones. Another might have never had a consistent policy at all, leaving behind a folder of photos shot on five different phone models across a decade of service calls. None of that was a problem while each business had its own separate, smaller website with its own separate, lower customer expectations.

Merge those libraries into one unified brand site, though, and the inconsistency becomes obvious immediately. A visitor scrolling through a "recent work" gallery sees a crisp, well-lit before/after pair next to a dim, low-resolution phone photo from a different acquired company, and reads that gap as evidence of inconsistent work quality — even when the actual craftsmanship was identical. Photo quality functions as a trust signal in a services business where the customer usually can't evaluate the technical work directly, and an inconsistent gallery undermines the exact confidence a newly bundled brand is trying to build.

## Fix the Library, Don't Reshoot the History

Reshooting a decade of completed jobs across multiple acquired companies is not usually possible — the work is done, the client's property has moved on, and in a lot of cases the original technicians aren't even with the company anymore. The realistic fix is standardizing the existing files: consistent aspect ratio, consistent resolution, and consistent compression quality across every image in the merged gallery, regardless of which legacy company originally shot it.

This is a batch job, not a photo-by-photo cleanup. Sort the inherited photo library by source company first, since each source tends to have its own consistent quirks (one company's photos might all run slightly underexposed, another's might all be an unusual aspect ratio from an older phone model), then apply a standardized resize and compression pass to each source batch separately before combining everything into the unified site gallery. Trying to fix the whole merged pile image-by-image is slower and produces a less consistent result than fixing it by source first.

## Practical Steps for a Roll-Up's Marketing Team

1. **Audit the inherited photo libraries by source company** before merging them into the new brand's site — identify the worst offenders first.
2. **Standardize resolution and aspect ratio in a batch pass**, matched to whatever your new site's gallery template expects.
3. **Apply one compression quality setting across the entire combined gallery**, rather than leaving legacy files at whatever quality they originally shipped at.
4. **Set a photo standard going forward for the merged brand** — a simple one-page guide for technicians on lighting and framing prevents the next acquisition's photo library from creating the same problem again.

## What to Do With an Inherited Photo Library

If your company has just been rolled up into a larger multi-trade brand, or you're the one doing the rolling up, cleaning up the inherited photo library is a genuinely quick fix compared to almost anything else involved in a merger.
