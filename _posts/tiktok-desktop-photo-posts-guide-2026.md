TikTok's desktop web app now supports photo-mode posts, so you can build and publish a multi-image carousel directly from a browser instead of routing everything through the phone app first. That's a real workflow change, not a minor tweak — photo posts launched as a mobile-only format, and anyone managing a brand account from a laptop has been stuck exporting photos to a phone just to post them. That extra step is gone.

## What Changed and Why It Matters

Photo-mode posts — TikTok's answer to Instagram carousels — arrived in 2023 as a mobile-exclusive feature. Creating one meant opening the app on your phone, selecting up to 35 images from your camera roll, arranging them, and publishing from there. If your photos lived on a desktop (product shots from a shoot, screenshots, graphics exported from design software), you had to email or AirDrop them to your phone first.

The desktop web app closes that gap. You can now go to tiktok.com, start a new post, select photo mode, and upload images straight from your computer's file system. For anyone running a business account, managing client content, or batch-scheduling posts from a desktop workflow, this removes a genuinely annoying bottleneck.

Separately, TikTok added Voice typing for photo post captions on iOS, so you can dictate caption text instead of typing it on a phone keyboard. It's a smaller addition, but worth knowing if you're finishing a post on mobile after prepping the images on desktop.

## The Photo Specs That Actually Matter

TikTok photo posts use the same vertical frame as video: **1080x1920 pixels, a 9:16 aspect ratio**. That's the target for every image in a carousel, not just the cover photo. Upload something in a different ratio and TikTok will crop or pad it to fit, which is how you end up with awkward letterboxing or a subject cropped out of frame.

File size matters more than most people assume. TikTok recompresses every photo on upload no matter what you send it — there's no way to opt out of that pass. What you control is the input. A few things follow from that:

- **A huge, unoptimized original gives TikTok more to compress, which means more visible quality loss.** A 15MB camera-original JPEG and a properly compressed 1-2MB version at the same resolution will not look the same after TikTok's pipeline processes them — the smaller, already-optimized file tends to hold detail and color better because it isn't being crushed as hard a second time.
- **Oversized files also slow down the upload itself**, which matters more on desktop where you're often uploading several images in one carousel at once.
- **Matching dimensions across a carousel keeps the scroll experience consistent.** If one photo is 1080x1920 and the next is 1200x1600, viewers see a visible jump in framing as they swipe — TikTok doesn't auto-normalize carousel images to match each other.

The practical target: 1080x1920, sRGB color, and each file compressed down to a few megabytes without visible quality loss. That's small enough to upload fast and large enough that TikTok's recompression doesn't have to work overtime.

## How to Post a Photo Carousel From Desktop

1. **Prep your images first, not during upload.** Gather every photo you plan to include in the carousel before you open TikTok's upload screen — sorting order gets easier when everything's already correctly sized and sitting in one folder.
2. **Resize every image to 1080x1920.** Mixed dimensions are the most common reason a carousel looks inconsistent once it's live. Use a batch resize tool so you're not manually adjusting each file one at a time.
3. **Compress each photo to a few megabytes.** This is the step people skip, and it's the one that most affects how the post actually looks after TikTok processes it.
4. **Go to tiktok.com and start a new post.** Select photo mode from the upload options — it now sits alongside video upload in the desktop web app.
5. **Upload your prepped images directly from your computer.** No phone transfer required. Arrange them in the order you want the carousel to display.
6. **Add your caption, cover photo selection, and any music or text overlays**, then review the carousel preview before publishing — this is your last check that dimensions and cropping look right.
7. **Publish.** If you're finishing captions on mobile afterward, Voice typing is available on iOS if you'd rather dictate than type.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you post TikTok photo carousels from a computer now?

Yes. As of mid-2026, TikTok's desktop web app supports photo-mode posts with the same multi-image carousel format that was previously limited to the mobile app, so you can upload directly from tiktok.com without transferring files to a phone first.

### What size should photos be for a TikTok photo post?

Use 1080x1920 pixels in a 9:16 vertical aspect ratio, the same frame TikTok uses for video, and keep each file to just a few megabytes so it uploads quickly and holds up better through TikTok's automatic recompression.

### Does TikTok compress photos after you upload them?

Yes, TikTok recompresses every image on upload regardless of the original file size, which is why starting with a properly sized, already-compressed photo preserves more visible quality than uploading a large unoptimized original and letting TikTok do all the compression work itself.
