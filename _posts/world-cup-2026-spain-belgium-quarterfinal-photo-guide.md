**Spain beat Belgium 2-1 in the quarterfinal on July 10, and the winning goal wasn't a set piece or a training-ground move — it was Mikel Merino pouncing on a loose ball after backup goalkeeper Senne Lammens spilled a shot from Pau Cubarsi in the 88th minute.** Fabian Ruiz had put Spain ahead in the 30th, Charles De Ketelaere leveled it for Belgium in the 41st, and then the match sat in tension for nearly 50 minutes before a goalkeeping mistake decided it. That's the kind of goal almost nobody in the stands actually catches cleanly, and it's worth understanding why.

## Why a Rebound Goal Beats Your Reaction Time

A penalty, a corner, a free kick — these are moments you can prepare for. You know roughly when the ball is going to be dangerous, so you point your camera and wait. A rebound off a spilled save is the opposite: it depends on a goalkeeper making a mistake that nobody, including the goalkeeper, expected to make. There's no windup. The shot happens, the save attempt happens, the ball squirts loose, and a converted rebound can take under two seconds from spill to net.

By the time most fans process "the keeper dropped it," the ball is already in the goal. This isn't a reflexes problem you can train away with a faster phone — it's a structural mismatch between how predictable most goals are and how genuinely random this one was.

## How to Actually Be Ready for the Unpredictable Kind

1. **Shoot continuous burst any time the ball is near either box in the final 15 minutes of a tied match.** You can't predict a rebound, but you can raise the odds of your shutter already being open when one happens by staying in shooting mode through every dangerous moment, not just the ones that look scripted.
2. **Keep your frame wide enough to include the goalkeeper and the space in front of them**, not zoomed tight on a single attacker. A rebound develops in the gap between keeper and ball — crop tighter afterward if you need to, but don't shoot so tight live that the actual moment happens outside your frame.
3. **Don't lower your phone after the initial shot or save attempt.** The instinct is to relax once the "shot" part of the sequence looks over — that's precisely when a spilled save creates the moment that actually matters.
4. **Accept that some goals genuinely aren't catchable live, and lean on official highlights for the exact instant if you missed it.** Your own shot of the celebration immediately after is still a real, personal record of being there — it doesn't have to be the scoring moment itself to be worth keeping.

## The Takeaway From Late Drama Like This

Merino's winner is the kind of goal that separates fans who caught it from fans who only saw it on the replay screen, and the difference usually isn't reflexes — it's whether your camera was already up and shooting wide when the mistake happened. Stay in burst mode through the tense stretches of a tied knockout match, keep your frame wide enough to include the accident, and crop down afterward rather than betting everything on a tight shot of where you expected the goal to come from.
