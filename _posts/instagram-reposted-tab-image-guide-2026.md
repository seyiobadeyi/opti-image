**Instagram's repost feature just got a real upgrade: you can now repost any public Reel or grid post from another account, and it lands in a dedicated Reposted tab on your profile instead of mixing into your main grid.** The original creator stays credited, your followers can tap through to the source, and you can't edit or crop what you're reposting — it shows up exactly as the original poster shared it. It's Instagram formally catching up to what Twitter/X, TikTok, and Threads have all had for years: a low-friction way to pass along something you didn't make without pretending you did.

## Why the Separate Tab Is the Actual News

The mechanics of reposting aren't new — people have been screenshotting and reposting on Instagram informally forever. What's new is that Instagram built a structural home for it that doesn't touch your main grid. That's a deliberate design choice, and it changes the psychology of what belongs where. Your main grid was already the curated version of your feed; now it's explicitly the only version of your feed, since anything you didn't create or personally choose to feature goes to a separate space by default.

Practically, that means the photos you do post to your main grid carry a little more signal than they did last month. A grid that used to be diluted with reposted memes and reshared content is now, structurally, just your stuff — which raises the bar slightly for what you decide is worth putting there.

## The Part That Affects Your Own Photos, Not Just What You Repost

Here's the angle most coverage of this feature is missing: if reposting is now this easy and this normalized, your own posts are more likely to get reposted by other people too — pulled into their Reposted tab, shown to their followers, potentially seen well beyond your own audience. A repost carries forward whatever version of the image is already live on the platform. It doesn't re-upload your original file; it references the post as it already exists, compression included.

That means the usual advice about posting the highest-quality version of a photo you can matters slightly more now. If your original post went up already softened by heavy compression on your end before upload — a common problem with photos taken on older phones or exported at low settings from editing apps — that's the version that circulates every time someone reposts it. There's no second chance to fix it once it's out there.

## What to Actually Do Differently

1. **Treat your main grid photos as final, not draft.** Since reposts no longer dilute your grid, what you post there is unambiguously "the photo you chose" — worth a quick check before posting instead of after.
2. **Upload at the highest quality Instagram will accept, not the smallest file that clears.** Instagram compresses on its end regardless, but starting from a strong source file gives its compression more to work with and produces a cleaner result than starting from an already-softened image.
3. **Before reposting someone else's content, remember you can't fix it.** No cropping, no editing — if the framing or quality bothers you, that's staying exactly as-is on your profile.
4. **If you're posting content you expect to get reposted a lot, size it deliberately.** A photo optimized to look sharp at typical feed display sizes holds up better through however many reposts it ends up going through than one exported carelessly.
