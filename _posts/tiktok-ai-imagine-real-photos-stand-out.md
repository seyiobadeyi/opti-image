**TikTok just put AI image generation one tap away from every user with its new AI Imagine feature in the Create tab, and that changes what "looks real" means on the platform going forward.** This isn't a niche AI art tool anymore — it's built into the same creation flow people use to post a selfie or a product shot. Within a few months, a meaningful share of the images in anyone's For You feed will be generated, not captured. That's a bigger deal for sellers and photographers than it sounds, and most of them aren't thinking about it yet.

## The Shift: From "Is This Edited?" to "Is This Real At All?"

For the last several years, the trust question around photos was about editing — was this filtered, retouched, staged? AI Imagine pushes the question one level further back: was this photo taken at all? Once generated images are common enough in a feed, viewers stop assuming a photo is real by default. They start actively looking for tells — the too-perfect lighting, the slightly-off hand, the background that doesn't quite make sense — before they trust what they're looking at.

That skepticism cuts both ways. It's bad news if you're trying to pass off a generated image as real. It's good news if you have an actual, real photo of an actual, real product — because a genuine photo now carries more weight than it used to, not less. Buyers who've been burned by AI-generated "product shots" that don't match what arrives in the box are getting sharper at spotting the difference, and they reward sellers who show the real thing.

## Where This Bites Sellers and Photographers Right Now

E-commerce listings are the clearest case. A generated product image can look flawless in ways a real photo of a physical object never quite does — no dust, no shadow in the wrong place, no slight asymmetry. That flawlessness is starting to work against sellers who use it, because shoppers increasingly read "too perfect" as a red flag rather than a selling point. A real photo, with the small imperfections that come from photographing an actual object under actual light, now signals honesty in a way it didn't have to a year ago.

Portfolio photographers face a version of the same problem from the other direction: clients want proof the work is real, and a photo that's technically excellent can get mistaken for AI output if it's presented without context. The fix isn't to make your photos look worse — it's to make sure the real ones are unmistakably, verifiably real, and that they actually load properly instead of sitting behind a slow spinner that makes anyone give up before they can judge for themselves.

## How to Make Sure Your Real Photos Actually Read as Real

1. **Keep your EXIF data intact on anything you're using as proof of authenticity.** Metadata showing a real camera, a real timestamp, and real GPS coordinates is a quiet but genuine signal — strip it only for privacy-sensitive posts, not for your primary product or portfolio shots.
2. **Don't over-retouch.** The instinct to smooth out every imperfection is exactly what makes a real photo start looking synthetic. Light color correction is fine; erasing every shadow and texture works against you now.
3. **Compress for speed, not just file size.** A real photo that loads slowly gets judged less favorably than a fast-loading one, full stop — viewers don't wait around to decide something is trustworthy. [Compressing your images](/compress) properly means they show up instantly, imperfections and all, which is the point.
4. **Show the same product from multiple real angles.** Generated images are usually one flawless hero shot. A seller with five photos of the same physical object from different angles, in different light, is much harder to fake convincingly — and buyers know it.
5. **Use consistent, real backgrounds across a shop or portfolio.** A generator can produce a new "perfect" background for every image; a real photographer's actual studio setup shows up consistently across a body of work, and that consistency itself reads as authentic.

## This Isn't Going Away

TikTok isn't the only platform pushing generation into the default creation flow, and it won't be the last. The direction is clear: generated images get cheaper and more common, and the value of a genuinely real, well-shot, fast-loading photo goes up as a result — not down. Sellers and photographers who lean into that now, rather than trying to compete with generators on polish, are the ones who'll benefit most as feeds get more synthetic.

## The Takeaway

- AI-generated images are becoming a normal part of everyday feeds, not a novelty
- Real photos gain trust value from that shift, but only if they're presented as unmistakably real — imperfect, properly attributed, and fast-loading
- A slow-loading real photo wastes the one advantage it has over a generated one
