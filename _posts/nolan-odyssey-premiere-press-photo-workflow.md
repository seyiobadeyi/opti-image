**Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey opened July 17 with the kind of press tour that generates thousands of photos in a single week — red carpet arrivals, junket portraits, behind-the-scenes unit stills — and almost none of the coverage of the film itself mentions the image pipeline that gets all of it in front of the public on the same day it's shot.** It's worth looking at, because the same compression and delivery discipline studios apply at a massive scale is exactly what any smaller event photographer or organizer needs too.

## The Scale of a Major Press Tour

A cast this size — Matt Damon, Tom Holland, Zendaya, Robert Pattinson, Anne Hathaway — touring for a single film means parallel photo shoots happening across multiple cities and events in the same week: red carpet premieres, individual press junket portrait sessions, group cast photos, behind-the-scenes unit photography from the production itself. Each of these produces its own volume of raw images that need to be culled, edited, and distributed on a tight same-day or next-day schedule to match press embargoes and release-date coverage.

## Why Press-Kit Photos Aren't Distributed at Full Quality

Studios have a specific reason for capping the resolution and format of officially distributed press images rather than handing out full camera-original files: it's a licensing and control mechanism as much as a technical one. Lower-resolution, watermarked, or otherwise controlled press assets are harder to repurpose beyond their intended editorial use, protect embargo timing across regions with staggered release dates, and still give outlets more than enough quality for standard print and digital publication.

That's a deliberate tradeoff between accessibility and control that most readers never think about — the polished photo running in a hundred publications on release day is a carefully sized and formatted deliverable, not a raw file straight off a camera.

## What This Looks Like at a Smaller Scale

The same principle scales down to any event photographer or organizer managing their own photo distribution, just without the licensing complexity. A wedding photographer, a conference organizer, or anyone running a public event faces a version of the same problem: getting photos out fast, at a consistent quality and format, without either overwhelming recipients with oversized files or under-delivering on quality.

1. **Standardize your export resolution and format before the event**, not photo by photo afterward — consistency across a whole gallery matters more than any single image being maximally sharp.
2. **Compress for the actual delivery channel.** A photo headed to a printed program needs different handling than one going into a same-day online gallery.
3. **Keep the full-resolution originals archived separately** from whatever compressed version gets distributed, the same way a studio keeps camera-original files distinct from press-kit assets.
4. **Batch process rather than handling images one at a time** when volume is high — a same-day turnaround requirement doesn't leave room for manually exporting hundreds of files individually.
