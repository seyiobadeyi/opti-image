## Where the Quality Actually Gets Lost

A broadcast or stream is already compressed before it reaches your screen — that's normal and unavoidable. The damage happens after: a phone photo of a TV screen adds moiré patterns and glare on top of the broadcast's own compression, and a screen recording or capture on a streaming app frequently gets recompressed again by whatever app you use to trim and share the clip. By the time a screenshot has gone through two or three of these passes, fine detail in a facial expression or on-screen caption is often the first thing to go soft, which is exactly the detail a reaction screenshot depends on to land.

## Getting a Clean Screenshot on Premiere Night

1. **Capture directly from the streaming app's screen-record or screenshot function if you're watching via Peacock or a streaming device, rather than photographing a TV screen with your phone** — this skips the moiré and glare problem entirely.
2. **Crop tight to just the reaction moment** rather than keeping the full frame. A smaller crop means more of your remaining file size budget goes toward the pixels that actually matter for the shot.
3. **Compress deliberately, once, at the quality level you choose** — instead of letting three different apps in the sharing chain each apply their own automatic recompression on top of each other.
4. **Add captions or text as a separate final step** on the already-cropped and already-compressed image, not baked in earlier where it'll degrade along with everything else in later compression passes.
