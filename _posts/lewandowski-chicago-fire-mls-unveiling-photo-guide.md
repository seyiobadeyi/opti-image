**Robert Lewandowski has agreed to join Chicago Fire as a free agent after his contract at Barcelona expired, giving MLS one of the biggest names to arrive in the league this year.** Behind every signing like this — a genuine marquee name switching continents and leagues — is a same-day photo and content operation that has to move fast, because a club only gets one moment where a name this big becomes officially, photographably theirs.

## Why Signing Day Is a Photography Sprint, Not a Casual Shoot

The moment a deal is finalized, a club typically has a matter of hours before it wants the announcement live everywhere — the team website, every social channel, press wires, and often a stadium concourse banner or LED board for that week's home game. All of that content usually traces back to one short jersey-reveal photo session: the player holding up the new shirt, maybe a scarf, a handshake with the sporting director, a few posed shots against the club's branded backdrop. That single session then has to become dozens of differently-cropped, differently-formatted deliverables within the same afternoon.

## Where the Format List Actually Gets Long

A club's digital team rarely needs just "the photo." A single Lewandowski-in-a-Chicago-Fire-shirt image typically has to become: a square crop for Instagram and X, a vertical crop for Stories and Reels, a wide crop for the team website hero banner, a cropped headshot version for the roster page, a high-resolution version for press wire distribution, and often a specifically-sized version for any in-stadium LED board or print program that week. Each of those has different aspect ratio and resolution requirements, and getting all of them out within the same news cycle — while the story is still the top item in soccer coverage — is the actual operational pressure, more than the shoot itself.

## The Workflow That Actually Keeps Up

1. **Shoot in the highest resolution available**, since every downstream crop pulls from the same source file — a signing photo that has to become ten different formats can't afford to start from a compressed original.
2. **Crop for the tightest, most demanding format first** (usually square or vertical for social), then work outward — it's easier to expand a well-composed crop's surrounding context than to rescue a bad crop.
3. **Batch-export every required size and format in one pass** rather than manually resizing for each platform as requests come in through the afternoon.
4. **Compress each exported version to that platform's practical target** — a press-wire file and an Instagram post have very different acceptable file sizes, and treating them identically wastes either quality or load time somewhere.

## Why This Matters Beyond One Signing

Every MLS club runs some version of this exact workflow every time it lands a name big enough to warrant a full rollout, and the clubs that handle it smoothly are the ones with a repeatable process rather than an improvised one each time. The technical bottleneck is never the photography — professional club photographers know how to get the shot. It's the fast, multi-format turnaround immediately afterward, on a deadline set by news cycles rather than the club's own schedule.
