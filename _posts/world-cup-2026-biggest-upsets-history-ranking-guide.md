**The biggest upset in World Cup history is still USA 1-0 England from 1950, and nothing in the 76 years since has fully dethroned it — though Norway's win over Brazil in this year's Round of 16 now sits comfortably in the top five.** The tournament is over, the trophy's been lifted, and this is the point in the cycle where the shocks that happened along the way get sorted against the ones that came before them. Some upsets fade into trivia. A handful become the moments people still reference when a new one happens. Here's how they stack up.

## 1. USA 1-0 England, 1950

Nothing else on this list comes close for pure improbability. England arrived at the 1950 World Cup in Brazil as one of the tournament favorites, the country that invented the sport, facing a United States team built mostly of part-time players — a mailman, a dishwasher, students — who had never been given a realistic chance. Joe Gaetjens' goal in the 37th minute stood up as the only score of the game, and the result was considered so implausible back home that several English newspapers reportedly assumed a wire-service typo and printed it as a 10-1 England win before corrections went out.

That's the bar. A team with essentially no professional infrastructure beating the birthplace of football, at the sport's own tournament, by a result nobody believed even after it happened. Everything else on this list is measured against that gap between expectation and outcome, and nothing has fully closed it since.

## 2. North Korea 1-0 Italy, 1966

Fifteen years later, a team from a country with almost no international football presence beat a two-time World Cup winner and sent them home in the group stage. North Korea's win over Italy at the 1966 tournament in England produced scenes that are still recognizable today — Italian players returning home to a hostile reception, and grainy footage of North Korean players celebrating a result their own federation had barely prepared the press for.

What separates this from other group-stage shocks is the gap in football infrastructure behind each team, not just the talent gap on the field. Italy had a professional league system that had already produced multiple World Cup titles. North Korea had almost no international competitive experience at that level. The distance between those two starting points is part of why this result still gets cited whenever a modern shock happens.

## 3. Cameroon 1-0 Argentina, 1990

Argentina arrived at Italia '90 as the defending champions with Diego Maradona still at the center of the team, and Cameroon beat them in the tournament opener. It wasn't a group-stage afterthought — it was the first match of the entire World Cup, played in front of a global audience tuning in specifically to watch the champions begin their title defense, and the champions lost.

Cameroon went on to become the first African team to reach a World Cup quarterfinal that same tournament, which retroactively made the opening result look less like a fluke and more like an announcement. But in the moment, on that single night, it was simply the defending champions losing their first match to a team most of the world hadn't taken seriously. Few upsets carry that specific combination of timing and stakes — the very first match, against the reigning champions, on the biggest possible stage for it to happen.

## 4. Senegal 1-0 France, 2002

This is the closest historical parallel to what a defending-champion upset looks like from the inside, and it happened in the exact same slot Cameroon's did twelve years earlier: the tournament opener. France, champions from 1998, had Zinedine Zidane and a squad considered among the most talented ever assembled, and Senegal — playing in their first-ever World Cup — beat them 1-0 to open the 2002 tournament.

France didn't just lose the opener; they went on to be eliminated in the group stage without scoring a single goal in the entire tournament, which turned Senegal's win from a shocking start into the defining image of France's title defense collapsing. Of everything on this list, Senegal-France is the one that most closely mirrors the shape of Norway's 2026 win over Brazil: a heavily favored, historically dominant football nation, beaten by a team playing with nothing to lose.

## 5. Norway 1, Brazil 0 — Round of 16, 2026

Brazil came into this year's tournament as a five-time champion and one of the favorites to add a sixth star, and Erling Haaland's Norway sent them home in the Round of 16. It's the biggest shock of the 2026 tournament by a wide margin, and it's not close among this year's other results — the closest competitor was Morocco eliminating co-host Canada in the same round, and that doesn't carry the same weight as toppling a five-time champion.

Where does it actually rank historically? Below USA-England for sheer improbability, since Norway arrived with a genuine world-class player in Haaland and a real footballing pedigree, unlike the 1950 USA squad. Below Senegal-France as pure stakes theater, because Brazil wasn't the reigning champion the way France was in 2002. But it sits comfortably alongside North Korea-Italy and Cameroon-Argentina — a result that will get replayed and referenced every time a new shock happens, for as long as people are still watching this sport. We covered the moment itself in detail in [Norway's win over Brazil and what it did to stadium wifi](/blog/world-cup-2026-norway-brazil-upset-photo-sharing) back when it happened; with the tournament finished, it holds up as a genuine top-five World Cup upset, not just a 2026 headline.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the biggest upset in World Cup history?

USA's 1-0 win over England at the 1950 World Cup is widely considered the biggest upset in the tournament's history, since a team of amateurs and part-timers beat the country that invented the sport in a result so unlikely that some English newspapers initially assumed it was a wire error for 10-1.

### How does Norway beating Brazil in 2026 compare to past World Cup upsets?

Norway's win over Brazil in the 2026 Round of 16 ranks among the biggest upsets in World Cup history, comparable to North Korea beating Italy in 1966 or Cameroon beating Argentina in 1990, though it sits just below results like USA-England 1950 and Senegal-France 2002 that carried even higher stakes or a bigger gap in stature.

### Why do old World Cup upset photos still get shared decades later?

Iconic upset photos — the scoreboard, the players' reactions, the crowd — keep circulating for decades because they capture a moment nobody expected, which makes them far more shareable than routine match photos.
