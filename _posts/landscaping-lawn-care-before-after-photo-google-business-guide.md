**A landscaping or lawn care company's Google Business Profile photos do more for new business than almost anything else in its marketing, and most of those photos are shot with zero consistency between the before and after shots that are supposed to sell the work.** The fix isn't a better camera. It's matching the angle, framing, and lighting between the two shots and getting the file size right before it goes up — two things that cost nothing and that almost nobody in this industry actually does.

## Why the Photo Section Carries More Weight Than the Reviews

A homeowner comparing three landscaping companies in their area is almost always looking at the same three things: star rating, a few reviews, and photos. Reviews take time to read and can feel generic across competitors. Photos are instant. A scroll through a company's before/after gallery tells a homeowner in five seconds whether this business does the kind of work they want — which is exactly why a sloppy, inconsistent photo set undersells work that might genuinely be excellent.

## The Matching Problem Nobody Fixes

Most landscaping crews take the "after" photo the moment a job wraps, standing wherever's convenient. The "before" photo, if it exists at all, was usually taken weeks earlier on a walkthrough, from a different spot, at a different time of day, sometimes on a different phone entirely. Put those two side by side and the comparison does half the work it should — a viewer has to mentally correct for the angle change before they can even register the actual transformation.

Fixing this costs nothing but a habit change. Stand in the same spot for the before shot as you plan to stand for the after. Note the time of day and try to match it, since shadows and light shift what a lawn or garden bed actually looks like. If a specific tree or fence post is in frame for one, keep it in frame for both — it gives the viewer a fixed reference point that makes the change register instantly.

## The Upload Mistake That's Just as Common

Modern phone cameras produce huge files — often 4 to 8 megabytes per photo. Uploading that straight to a Google Business Profile listing without resizing doesn't make the photo look better on a homeowner's screen; Google compresses and resizes it for display regardless. What it does do is slow down every page load where those photos appear, on a listing that's overwhelmingly being viewed on someone's phone while they're standing outside deciding who to call.

1. **Resize before uploading**, not after — a 1600px-wide image displays identically to a full-resolution original in a Business Profile gallery while loading dramatically faster.
2. **Compress the before and after photos to the same quality setting**, so one doesn't look noticeably sharper or muddier than the other purely because of file handling.
3. **Group before/after pairs together** in the upload order rather than scattering them across a general photo dump — most profiles let you organize by category, and "before & after" deserves to be its own section.
4. **Replace or add photos monthly**, prioritizing the most recent seasonal work — a gallery that's clearly current signals an active, in-demand business more than an old one, no matter how good the old photos were.

## What to Do This Week

Pick your next three jobs and commit to standing in the same spot for the before and after shot on each one. Resize and compress them to match before they go up. That's the entire fix, and it costs a landscaping business nothing except the five minutes it takes to actually mark where you were standing.
